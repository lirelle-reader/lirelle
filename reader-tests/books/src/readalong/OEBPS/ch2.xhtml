The Second Tide

The tide tables were printed in a town far inland, and it showed. The boy from the chandlery ran errands for anyone who paid in toffee. Marta kept the window open a hand's width, whatever the weather. A coil of wire, a tin of grease and a spare wick sat on the sill. Whatever the answer was, it would have to wait for the morning boat. There were three keys on the hook and only two doors they fitted.

Nobody trusted the barometer, least of all the man who owned it. The stairs of the tower numbered one hundred and twelve, not counting the ladder. Old Anselm swore the fog had a colour, though he could never name it. Two fishermen argued gently about a net neither of them owned. A kettle whistled somewhere behind the net loft, thin and patient. Somewhere a shutter banged twice, then settled against its latch.
