The Barometer

Nobody trusted the barometer1, and the harbour got on well enough without it.

The ledger2 named the lamp3, the chart4 and the tide5; it forgot a key6 and a rope7, told the long story8 and the longer one9.

The tide table cites itself10, and so does the almanac11; the whole story12 is elsewhere, and the chart alone13 too.

The net14, the oar[15] and the bell16 came back; the anchor17, the rope18, the gate19 and 20 did not, nor the press21.

Two fishermen argued gently about a net neither of them owned. Nobody in Port Vesper remembered who had painted the lighthouse green. A rope creaked against the piling with the rhythm of slow breathing. The gulls returned at dusk, all of them this time, loud as a quarrel. The stairs of the tower numbered one hundred and twelve, not counting the ladder.
